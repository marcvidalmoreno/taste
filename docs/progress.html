At a glance
  • Where it stands: 27 written styles, each one a prompt an image model can use. A local web app to rate images. A loop that turns ratings into style edits.
  • Biggest shift: on 13 Nov, styles stopped being colour clusters and started being things a person can recognise by eye. Colour alone guesses the right style only 36 % of the time.
  • Sources: about 1,000 saved wallpapers and the ART collection (828 unique images). The reference images stay on the laptop; only the written styles are public.
  • The loop: generate (Codex, one fresh run per image) → rate keep / almost / no, with a reason → distil every ~20 ratings → Marc approves the style edits → rebuild.

Where this is going · ideas, not decided

Today the system learns slowly and only when asked: ratings pile up, and every ~20 a distil proposes edits. The next steps make that loop tighter and more of a conversation:

  • Reasons become rules. The "why" written next to each rating ("too busy", "too dark") is the richest signal. Repeated reasons can become avoid-list entries without waiting for a full distil.
  • Looks that earn their place. Each style now rotates several looks. Track which looks get kept and show those more often, while still trying the others now and then: explore, then exploit.
  • Crossing styles. Combine the medium of one style with the light of another, and let ratings say which pairings work. Recombination is where new styles come from.
  • New styles from the inbox. Loose images that fit no style pile up as candidates. When enough of them look alike, they become a proposed style.
  • A conscience built in. The copy check, run on every image, keeps borrowing honest: a manner taken, never a picture.
  • Others' taste. "Make your own taste" already lets anyone fork the repo and start from their own folder. Two tastes could also meet in one image.

The idea behind it

Art is putting things together: taking from here and there, filtered through your own taste and perspective.

That is what this repo does in slow motion. Collections are the "from here and there". Tagging by eye and the ratings are the taste. The written styles are the perspective, made explicit so a machine can share it. The AI does the copying and measuring at speed. The human decides what is worth keeping. That split, made visible and kept small enough to steer, is how the two of you get to express something together.
