Start here

The app is where you look at images, react to them and shape your styles. Every thumb, remix or style change feeds the written styles that each new image is built from. It runs on your laptop only, at http://127.0.0.1:8777; to open it, tell Claude "open taste".

Three things hold everywhere:

  • Everything counts at once. A thumb is saved the moment you press it. There is no Save button, and pressing the same thumb again takes it back.
  • Nothing spends until you say so. Runs, remixes and variations show what they cost in Codex allowance before you press Start or Make.
  • Every style change can be undone. From the message right after, or later from the style's card on the Taste page.

The loop, in one line: make images → react → the style learns → make better images.

Batches (home page)

A batch is one folder of images made together. This page lists them all, newest first, grouped by day (Today, Yesterday, then dates). The heading says how many images are left to rate.

Now and next

At the top while something is running, or for 30 minutes after it finished:

  • A card with Now or Just finished, the kind of run, its name, a progress bar and the subject being made.
  • Buttons: Open (or Rate them when done), Log, and Stop while running.
  • Next: the runs waiting in the queue, each with ↑ / ↓ to change the order and Remove.

Runs the usage limit cut short

When Codex hits its usage limit mid-queue, every run after it stops with nothing or half made. Below Now and next, one line then says how many runs and images that left, with the cost. Resume all queues them all again, oldest first, each in its own folder with the same prompts and numbers. Images blocked by the filter are not counted: they would only fail again. The line hides once nothing waits.

Images the safety filter blocked

A second line counts the images ChatGPT's safety filter blocked that no other model has tried yet. Pick a model in the small menu (FLUX on Cloudflare, or one of the models on this laptop; the app remembers it) and press Try with …: the same prompts, one run per batch; each image lands in its own batch, where it failed. Each model has its own rules, so some may be refused again. With FLUX, until Cloudflare is connected the button reads Connect Cloudflare first and opens New run.

Finding a batch

  • Search by batch name or style.
  • Kind: All, Batches, Variations & remixes, Single, each with a count.
  • Only with images to rate hides finished batches.

The filters are remembered while the tab is open, so coming back from a batch finds the list as you left it.

Each row

A strip of thumbnails, the name, the kind, date, number of images and styles, and on the right what is left: "4 to rate", "rated", "making now", or "2 ignored". The whole row is one click.

A batch

Click a row to open the batch: a grid of thumbnails and everything about how they were made.

Filters

  • All (where a batch opens): every image except the ignored ones. Rating changes the thumb on the tile and nothing moves.
  • To rate: images without a thumb. Images you rate during this visit stay here, so nothing jumps under your cursor; they move to Rated next time you open the batch. The number next to it drops as soon as you rate, so it always shows what is really left.
  • Rated: images with a thumb.
  • Ignored: images you hid. Only there when the batch has some.

Folder line

  • Where the images are on disk, with Copy path, Open folder and Download all: the full-size images as one zip. On a filter it reads Download these N and holds only the images the grid shows.
  • Change the style… when the batch has one style.
  • New run with this style (or these styles): opens New run with them picked.

Where it came from and where it went

  • Made from: for a remix or variations, the original image with a link back, and what you asked for.
  • Style: the batch's style (or styles), each a link to its card on Taste. A style you made from your own images shows them small after started from; click one to see it full size.
  • How it was made (folded): when and from where it started, styles, shape, wildcard, changes, the subjects per style and any direction you gave.
  • Made from images here: remixes and variations made from this batch, with links.

A batch that stopped early

If a run stopped before the end (Codex hit its usage limit, 3 failures in a row, or you pressed Stop), a line above the grid says how many images were never made and why. Finish this batch makes them with the same subjects, looks and kept examples as the first run, and shows the cost next to the button. It hides while a run for this batch is running or queued. Remixes, variations and single images that the usage limit cut short get the same line.

Tiles

  • Each tile shows the number, the style and the kind of change. A thumb badge shows once it is rated, and your why appears under it.
  • Up and Down buttons under each tile let you rate without opening the image.
  • While a run is making this batch, placeholder tiles say "Making…" (with the subject) or "Waiting", and turn into images as they land.
  • A tile without an image says exactly why, read from Codex's log: Blocked by the safety filter (with what it flagged, and whether it refused the prompt itself, where a retry gives the same answer, or the finished image, where a retry sometimes passes), Codex hit its usage limit, or Codex timed out (over 15 minutes). Then Retry once or Copy prompt; a blocked image also offers Try with and a model menu (the same prompt with FLUX or a local model, free).
  • Every tile has a small badge for the model that made it: Codex, FLUX, FLUX local, Z-Image, Playground, DreamShaper, Juggernaut, ZavyChroma or Animagine. Under the folder line, Made with names the batch's model in full.
  • For variations, a Seed tile shows the original first.

Under each tile, Publish picks the image for the public gallery site (press again to take it back); it stays there after you rate.

Keyboard in the grid: arrow keys move between tiles, U / D rate the focused one, P publishes it, I ignores it, Enter opens it.

Large view

Click any tile. The image fills the left, a panel on the right holds everything about it. Browser Back or Esc closes it.

Top bar

Batches (back to the home page) › the batch name (back to the batch), "3 of 12", "Hold S to see the seed" (variations only), Download (saves the full-size image, named after its batch), 1 : 1 to see real pixels and scroll around (press again, or click the image, to fit it back) and Full screen (image only; Esc leaves it). The image always opens fitted to the window, whole. ‹ and › step through the images.

Panel, top to bottom

  1. Number, style and kind.
  2. Thumbs and why, with a line saying what your thumb did (see Rating).
  3. Actions: Keep as example (X, see Taste), Remix… (R or V), Change the style… (E), Note for Claude… (C), and Ignore (I) or Restore when it has no thumb.
  4. More like this: the 8 existing images that look most like this one, by colour and framing (not by subject). Free: nothing is made. Same style only narrows it to this style. Click one to open it in the Gallery large view and step through the row with ← →; that view has its own row, so you can follow a look from image to image. ← Previous image (or Esc, Backspace) walks back the same way, one image at a time, until you are where you started. The first time takes a few seconds while every image is measured once.
  5. How it got here: every earlier version, oldest first, ending with this image. Each step says how it was made (Batch with its style, Remix, Variations, or a reference image you gave), what you asked for (the remix words or the variation's change), your thumb on it, and where it is. Click a step to open it in the Gallery large view; ← → then flick through the versions in order, so you see what each change did. ← Previous image brings you back. An image with no earlier version gets one quiet line instead, such as “made fresh in this batch (Dark cosmic glow), no earlier versions”; a remix whose source can't be found says “earlier version not found”. Below it, The style tells the style's side of the story: how it was created, the changes made to it before this image (your words, distils, edits with Claude), a dot for this image, and any change since, faded. Full history opens the style on the Styles page with its 👍 chart.
  6. Details: style with its palette, the change (for variations), how it was made, what was made from it (links), the seed, the size, when and how long it took.
  7. Prompt, the exact text sent to Codex, with Copy.

Hold S on a variation: the original replaces the image until you let go. Flicking between the two in place makes the difference jump out, better than looking left and right.

New run

Click New run in the header, or "New run" / "One image" on a style card or a batch. The form is remembered if you leave the page. On the right, This run stays in view: what will be made, what it costs and the Start button.

Batch

  1. Styles: click one or more (filter by name; Select all picks every style, or only the ones the filter shows; Clear resets).
  2. Experimental (off by default): try things the styles have not done yet. Codex writes bolder subjects (odd places, scales and pairings, a touch of the surreal), each image gets a new palette in place of the style's own, your ratings do not steer the picks, and every image after the first pushes one thing to an extreme. The batch name starts with "Experimental" and the change column shows newpal, so ratings tell which new palettes worked.
  3. Images: how many per style (1–8, then 10, 12, 16, 20 or 24) and the shape: Mixed (wide, portrait, square in turn, to see how a style holds up in each), Wide (16:9, desktop and Teams), Portrait (4:5, LinkedIn and phones) or Square (1:1).
  4. Wildcard (on by default, from 2 per style): the last image of each style pushes one thing to an extreme: light, framing, palette, scale, detail or mood.
  5. Subjects, one card per style:
    • Write one subject per line. Empty lines are written by Codex when the run starts (about 15 s per style), avoiding subjects used before.
    • Direction for Codex: a hint like "winter, at night" for the subjects it writes.
    • Write N now: Codex writes them now so you can read and edit them first.
    • Use examples: fills the empty lines with the style's own example subjects.
    • The card says how many are written and how many Codex will add.
  6. Name (optional): becomes the folder name after the date.

One image

  1. Pick a style (its palette and description show).
  2. Write the subject, or pick one of the style's example subjects.
  3. How many (1–10; same prompt, Codex varies the result) and the shape.

No style · palette only (first choice in the Style list): no style rules at all. Pick a palette (the palettes of your styles), write what you want in your own words, and Codex writes the whole prompt from them (about 15 s; Write the prompt now to read and edit it first, or just press Start). Picking another palette clears the prompt, since it names the old colours; changing the shape keeps it and only swaps its first sentence.

Before you start

  • Preview the prompts (free) (batch only): the exact prompts, without running Codex. Rows marked "example subject" get a fresh subject when the run starts.
  • When a picked style has images you kept as examples, a tick offers to send them along as references (on by default; up to 2 with each image, no extra images spent). The result then stays closer to those examples; untick it for a fresh start. The preview shows which images go with which prompt.
  • The cost block shows images, minutes and allowance (see Allowance).
  • Made with, at the top of the cost block (also in Remix and Try with): one of five models. Codex (best quality, closest to the style, uses the Codex allowance), FLUX (free, about 80 images a day on Cloudflare; good for drafts and simpler scenes), and three on this laptop, free and with no tokens: FLUX local, Z-Image, Playground, DreamShaper, Juggernaut, ZavyChroma and Animagine (see Models). The choice is remembered; a style with a default model (Models page) starts on it, marked "default", and your own pick holds after that.
    • With a local model the block shows the laptop time instead (about 1–3 minutes an image, plus a minute to load the model). ComfyUI starts by itself, hidden, and lets go of the memory after the run. New local runs get folders ending in -flux-local or the model's name (-zimage, -dreamshaper …).
    • The first time you pick FLUX, Connect Cloudflare asks for a free Cloudflare account, an API token (from the "Workers AI" template) and your account ID: paste the two and Save. They stay on this laptop; Disconnect forgets them.
    • With FLUX the block shows about how many images are left. Once Cloudflare's free images are used up it says so, with when that started and when the app last checked; the app checks every hour and the line changes when FLUX is back (Cloudflare's own reset time proved unreliable, so no time is promised). A run past the limit stops; the rest wait for Finish this batch.
    • Subjects you leave empty are still written by Codex (a small text job). "How it was made" on a batch says which model made it.
    • New FLUX runs get their own folder ending in -flux (for example 2026-11-23-winter-flux), and every FLUX image has a small FLUX badge on its tile, in batches and in the Gallery. Blocked images tried with FLUX stay in their own batch, next to the ChatGPT ones.
    • So FLUX stays close to the style, each FLUX image takes along 3 of your images of that style: kept examples first, then your newest thumbs-up images made by ChatGPT, a different three for each image. When a run already has an image to start from (Variations, kept examples ticked), FLUX uses that instead.
  • Start · make N images, or Add to queue when a run is busy. Ctrl + Enter does the same.

After Start you go to Batches; the batch opens by itself when its folder appears. Subjects and name are cleared, styles and settings are kept for next time.

Runs and queue

  • Runs go one at a time: batches, single images, remixes, variations and retries all wait in one queue. Runs started from chat show here too.
  • Watch progress in the header chip, the tab title, the Now and next card, and the placeholder tiles in the batch.
  • Log: the live output of the run and the queue below it.
  • Stop: asks first; the image being made is lost, the ones already made stay.
  • ↑ / ↓: moves a waiting run earlier or later in the queue.
  • Remove: takes a waiting run out of the queue.
  • When a run ends you get a message with Rate them or See them (or Details when nothing was made), and a desktop notice if you allowed it (the browser asks the first time). Messages about a failure stay until you close them with ×; every message has one.

Remix

More images from one you made, in its own style (this was Remix and Variations; they are one dialog now). Large view → Remix…, R or V. Nothing is spent until you press Make.

  1. Change: pick any of Palette, Subject, Light, Composition. Pick none and you get one of each (palette, subject, composition). Palette opens a panel: Surprise me (a different palette per image, suited to the style) or one of up to five palettes, with swatches: the style's other palettes (Style option 1, 2…) and ones taken from images of that style you liked (Liked image 1, 2…). The style's main palette is not offered, since the image already has it. With two or more changes, choose One change per image (you see what each does) or All changes in every image.
  2. Anything else?: words that go into every image. Under it, four cards of ideas "picked for this image" (click to add to the text, click again to take out; a ✓ and a count show what is used). More ideas opens all eight groups, Experimental included. The groups you use most come first.
  3. How close to the original: Close (the original goes along as a reference), Balanced (reference sent, free to move away), Loose (prompt only) or Wild ✦ (prompt only, asked to reinterpret boldly: expect misses and a few surprises). Z-Image can't take an image, so its set always works from the prompt only.
  4. How many images: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 or 12. The changes never move it. Fewer images than changes: only the first ones are made; more: they are shared out in turn.
  5. Made with: tick one or more models. Each makes the whole set in its own batch, with the model in its name, to compare. The style's usual model is ticked first; Local only limits the choice to this laptop.
  6. What you'll get: one tile per image, then a sentence saying what will be made, and one cost line per model. The two usage meters appear only when the five-hour window is over 70 % used.
  7. More options: Review the prompts before making (Codex writes one prompt per kind of image, about 15 s each and nothing spent; you read them with added words marked and removed ones struck through, and edit) or Write the prompt myself (start from the original prompt). An image with no prompt on record gets one written from the image first (about 30 s).

Each model's remix lands in its own batch, linked back to the original under "Made from", with the original as its Seed tile (hold S in the large view to compare). Older remix and variations batches look as they did.

Gallery

Every image ever made, in rows that fill the width without cropping, each with its 👍 / 👎 mark.

  • Find: type words to keep only the images whose prompt, why, style or batch holds all of them, in any order (for example red forest). The grid narrows as you type.
  • Rating: All, Up, Down, Almost (old), Not rated, each with a count.
  • ◉ Published: only the images you picked for the public gallery site (count beside it). Pick one with Publish (P) under a batch tile or in the large view; a small ◉ marks picked tiles. Only the image, its style name, date and subject go public, never the style text. Picks stay on this computer.
  • Batch menu.
  • Small / Medium / Large (end of the filter row): smaller images to see more at once, larger ones to judge them. The rows reflow at once, and the app remembers your choice.
  • Style tags (under the filters): one tag per style with its colours and count. Press a tag to show that style, press more to add them, press again to take one away; All styles clears them.
  • Model tags (under the style tags): the same for the model that made each image, Codex, FLUX, FLUX local, Z-Image, Playground, DreamShaper, Juggernaut, ZavyChroma, Animagine (and Local for the blind test). Every tile has the model's badge. A blind-test picture shows only in the blind batch, not again in its model's batch, so it stays blind; once every image in the blind batch is rated, its tiles show the real model and those marks count for it on the Models page.
  • The filters and the words are in the address, so Back and reload keep them.

Click an image for the large view. It is the same as the batch large view (see Large view): thumbs and a why, Keep as example, Remix…, Change the style…, Note for Claude…, More like this, How it got here, Download, 1 : 1, Full screen, the details with links to its style (on Taste) and its batch, and the prompt with Copy. ← → step through the Gallery as filtered. Two things stay in the batch view only: Ignore (the Gallery leaves ignored images out) and holding S for the seed. The grid keeps its order while you mark, so nothing jumps away; the filters apply again on the next visit.

The address follows the image you are on, for example #/gallery/2026-11-23-clone-lab/4-mood, so you can copy it, bookmark it or reload without losing your place; the filters stay in it too. The browser's Back button closes the large view.

Styles

Every style on one page, newest run first. Each row: its colours, name, the On / Off switch, how many images it made, 👍 rate with n (👍 out of 👍 + 👎, latest mark per image; greyed under 5 rated images, when it is only a hint), ★ kept as examples, the date of its last run and a small trend over its last rated batches. Find a style narrows the list; New style from images… beside it starts a new one from 1 to 4 pictures (New style from images).

  • Switch off (archive): the style is no longer offered in New run, in Suggestions or in the style shown for Remix. Its images stay in the Gallery and in the Overview chart (marked "archived"). It acts at once and says Undo. Switched-off styles are folded under Archived, here and at the bottom of Taste, each with Switch back on.
  • While a run uses a style, or Codex is still writing suggestions for it, its switch stays On with a 🔒 for those minutes (hover it to see why). Suggestions that are ready and waiting for you don't lock it: switching the style off sets them aside, and switching it back on brings them back. Styles that are off are dimmed as a whole row, under Archived.
  • Click a name to open its history under its row (one at a time; the address #/styles/<style> opens it directly). Evolution lists, oldest first, when it was created, each commit, each edit or suggestion applied from the app and each switch, and for each the 👍 rate of the images made since the event before it, then since this one, with n. The chart shows the 👍 rate per batch with the numbered events; Runs lists every batch with its 👍, 👎 and ★ and links to it. Reading history costs nothing.

Taste

The styles as they are written, each with your own images as evidence. Styles with the most images come first; the index at the top jumps to one. New style from images…, top right, starts a new one.

Each style card

  • New run, One image, Change this style…, Fork… and Note for Claude…. A forked style says Forked from its parent.
  • Changed from the app (folded): every edit made from the page, newest first, with your words, whether Codex or you wrote it, what changed field by field, and Undo.
  • How new runs lean (folded): which options your thumbs made more or less likely ("1.8× as likely"), with the ups and downs behind each, and the subjects you liked. Every option keeps a chance.
  • The description, Started from (the reference images, for a style made with New style from images; click one to see it large) and the palette with colour codes.
  • Light, Composition, Medium, Mood, Detail, Lean towards, Avoid, and Good for (desktop, Teams, phone, post).
  • Evidence: first the images you kept as examples (★), then up to 8 rated Up, 4 rated Down with their whys, how many are not rated, and a link to all of them in the Gallery. Every example has a small ☆ / ★: click it to keep it as an example of this style, or to stop. Kept examples are sent along as references when you start a new run of the style (see New run).

New style from images

From Taste or Styles → New style from images…. For a look you found elsewhere (a gallery, a book cover, a Midjourney style code's sample images) and want as a style of your own.

  1. Add 1 to 4 images: choose them, drop them on the box, or copy an image in the browser and paste it with Ctrl + V. Two to four with one look in common work best: Codex keeps what they share. × removes one.
  2. Optional: a name (Codex suggests one if empty) and what draws you to them ("the muted dusk colours, flat shapes"). Your words steer what Codex looks for.
  3. Propose the style: Codex looks at the images and writes the look in words (about 30 s). The palette is measured from the images' pixels, not guessed.
  4. Read it. Every field can be changed; in the lists, one line is one option, and light, composition and mood get one option picked per image. Nothing is written yet.
  5. Create style (or Ctrl + Enter). It is on the Taste page at once.
  6. First run opens New run with the style picked. Your thumbs then steer it like any other style.

The style describes the look, never an artist, studio or source. The images stay on your laptop: they are other people's work and never go to GitHub. Undo on the last step, or on the Taste card under "Changed from the app", takes the style off the page again (its file is set aside, not deleted). The new style file goes to your private GitHub repo only when you tell Claude "commit my style edits".

Fork a style

A new style that starts as an existing one, with the difference you describe (Robots → Night robots). The parent stays exactly as it is. From a Taste card → Fork…, or from Change the style → As a new style…, which carries the words you typed over.

  1. Give it a name and say what should be different (quick picks help).
  2. Propose the fork: Codex writes the variant (about 30 s, nothing is made yet). Or Copy as it is: the same style under a new name, no Codex, to edit yourself.
  3. Check every field. The words that differ from the parent are marked under each field; the palette says whether the colours changed, with a button to go back to the parent's.
  4. Create fork (or Ctrl + Enter). It is on the Taste page at once, with no images or ratings. First run shows whether the difference came through.

Undo on the last step, or on the Taste card under "Changed from the app", sets the fork's file aside (never deleted). The fork's card says "Forked from", and in Styles → History the fork's first event reads "Forked from" its parent and the parent's reads "Forked into" the fork. A switched-off style can't be forked. The new file goes to your private repo only when you tell Claude "commit my style edits".

Change a style

From the large view (E), the Gallery large view, a batch with one style, or a Taste card. The change applies to every image made in this style after you apply it.

With Codex

  1. Say what should change, or add quick picks: fewer elements, less repetition, more variety, simpler compositions, less busy detail, calmer with fewer colours. "How the style reads now" shows the current text.
  2. Propose the edit: Codex reads the whole style and proposes the smallest edit (about 15 s, nothing changes yet).
  3. Read it field by field, added words marked, removed ones struck through. Untick any field you want to leave as it is.
  4. Apply to the style (or Ctrl + Enter).

With your own words

Add my words as they are skips Codex: choose Lean towards ("one main subject, quiet areas around it") or Avoid ("rows of identical towers"), check, and apply.

After applying, the message offers Undo for a few seconds; later, use Undo on the Taste card. Your edits stay on your laptop until you tell Claude "commit my style edits".

Suggestions

Every 20 ratings Codex reads your latest thumbs and whys and suggests edits per style. It runs by itself; the header chip appears when a suggestion waits. Open it from the chip or Overview → Suggestions.

  • Each card shows the style, its ups and downs, up to 12 of the images you rated (with your whys, linked to the large view), what Codex read in them, and the proposed edit field by field.
  • Untick fields you disagree with, then Apply, or Skip the card. An applied card offers Undo.
  • Styles that need nothing are folded under "Fine as they are".
  • The round closes by itself once nothing waits. "Look now" asks early when a style has enough new ratings.

Notes for Claude

For feedback that doesn't fit a thumb or a field. From the large view (C), a Taste card, or General note… on Overview.

  1. Write it plainly: "faces look plastic in every run", "this composition is the one: more like it".
  2. Leave the note. The message offers to copy "process my app notes".
  3. In chat with Claude, say "process my app notes". Claude looks at what each note points at, proposes style edits as one set for your OK, and answers each note.
  4. Read the answers on Overview. A note not yet read can be taken back.

Codex never reads these notes.

Overview

  • Style suggestions: how many ratings until Codex looks again (a meter out of 20), a link to Suggestions, and Preview the distil summary (shows what Codex would read, changes nothing).
  • Notes for Claude: your notes with Claude's answers, Take back, and General note….
  • Per style: a bar per style with ups and downs, since the last look or all ratings, and the same numbers as a table.

Allowance

Images are made with Codex on the ChatGPT plan: no bill, but a shared allowance. Wherever something spends, a cost block shows:

  • Images, about a minute each, and the share of the five-hour window (about 0.25–0.75 % per image).
  • Meters for the five-hour window and the week, read from Codex's own log.
  • This Codex week: images made since Codex's weekly reset (FLUX images are free and not counted), what this run adds as a share of the week, and when the week resets. It warns when a run may reach the weekly limit.

Writing subjects, rewriting prompts and proposing style edits are short text jobs and don't make images. When the app runs in dry-run mode, a notice says so and nothing is spent.

Use cases

Browse old images, like one, and make the style follow it

  1. Gallery → pick a style, or Not rated to see what you never judged.
  2. Click an image and step with ← →.
  3. Found one you love? Press U right there. Add a why: "calm horizon, one subject".
  4. It counts at once: new runs of this style now lean toward this image.
  5. To write it into the style for good, click Change the style… and describe what you like in it: "one subject on a low horizon, wide empty sky". Check the proposal and apply.
  6. On Taste, the image shows among the style's Up examples, and your edit under "Changed from the app".

To keep one exact image as a reference example for its style, press X (or Keep as example) in the large view: it shows first on Taste and goes along as a reference in new runs of that style.

Start a style from a look you found

  1. Find 2 to 4 images with the look, for example a style code's samples on a gallery site. Copy each one (right-click → Copy image).
  2. Taste → New style from images…, paste each with Ctrl + V, and say in a line what you like in them.
  3. Propose the style, read it, fix a word if needed, Create style.
  4. First run, then thumbs. After about 20 ratings, Suggestions sharpen it.

Rate a new batch quickly

  1. When the run ends, click Rate them in the message.
  2. Open the first image and use only the keyboard: U or D, Enter for a why and to move on, → to skip.
  3. I hides a broken one.

Make images for a LinkedIn post

  1. New run → Batch, pick one or two styles.
  2. Shape Portrait or Square, 3 or 4 per style.
  3. Write your subjects, or a direction for Codex and Write N now to read them first.
  4. Preview the prompts (free) if you like, then Start. You'll get a message when they're ready.

One specific image in a style

  1. On the style's Taste card click One image.
  2. Write the subject, pick the shape, make 1–4.

An image is almost right

  1. Open it and press R.
  2. Say the change ("seen from far above, no people"), tick what must stay, keep the reference on.
  3. Write the new prompt, read the changes, make 1–2.

Find out what you like about an image

  1. Open it and press V and pick nothing (one palette, one subject, one framing), or tick Palette, Subject, Light and Composition.
  2. In the new batch, hold S on each to compare with the original.
  3. Thumb them: you see which single change worked, and the style learns it too.

Try an image in another style

  1. Open it and press V.
  2. Under Style, choose Another style, tick Palette, and start.

A style keeps doing something you dislike

  1. On one of its images press E.
  2. Add my words as they are → Avoid → "rows of identical towers" → apply.
  3. Changed your mind? Undo on the style's Taste card.

The suggestions chip appears

  1. Click it. Read each card: your images, Codex's reading, the edit.
  2. Untick what you disagree with, Apply, or Skip.

Feedback too fuzzy for a field

  1. Press C and write it plainly.
  2. Later, tell Claude "process my app notes"; the answers show on Overview.

An image failed

  1. In the batch, the No image tile: Retry once, or Copy prompt to try it elsewhere.
  2. Many missing because the run stopped (usage limit): wait for the limit to reset, then Resume all on Batches (every run at once), or Finish this batch above one batch's grid.
  3. Several runs failed: one message counts them all and says the reason when it's the same for all; Details lists each run with what it made and its log.
  4. The header says runs on hold: a script error would make every waiting run fail, so the queue waits instead. "Waits for a script fix" starts again by itself once it's fixed; "stopped on a script error" has a Continue button for when it's fixed.

Use an image or prompt outside the app

  1. In the batch: Open folder or Copy path.
  2. In the large view: Copy next to the prompt, for Gemini or ChatGPT.

Stop a run you started by mistake

  1. Header chip → Log → Stop, or Remove it if it is still waiting.
